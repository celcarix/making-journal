## Documentation 

This week was primarily spent further refining and confirming my project direction, and beginning to translate my ideas into more concrete forms. Through NotebookLM's drafting and evaluation, I gained a clearer understanding of the overall structure of the project statement, its current strengths, and areas requiring modification and improvement. I also corrected major issues in the original QR code interaction, making the overall functionality and interaction methods clearer, although some minor details still need further adjustment and refinement. Through peer feedback and round-robin discussions, I gained a better understanding of which parts were clearly expressed and which areas needed further development. In addition, I sought out new reference images, which helped me better understand the physical book format, layout arrangement, and the possibilities of physical books in terms of interaction and visual presentation, giving me a clearer idea of ​​my next production direction. During the sprint making process, I also began to transform my original notes into physical book layout drafts, further considering the possibilities of content arrangement and physical format. Overall, this week helped me gradually move from conceptual development to actual production, and also gave me a clearer understanding of the project direction for the next stage.

*Case study*

This case study made me realize that data and algorithms are not just used to analyse information, they can also directly influence human work allocation and decision-making. *NotebookLM - Drafting*
![alt text](../assets/week-09/image-1.png) 
While using NotebookLM's draft project statement, I found it very helpful in organizing my previously scattered ideas, making the entire project sound more structured, and also helping me to confirm my direction. However, I also noticed that some sentences were somewhat AI-like and the content was too broad, so I still need to revise them myself to make them more specific and more in line with the project I actually want to do.


*NotebookLM - Evaluation*
![alt text](../assets/week-09/image-4.png) 
Using NotebookLM helped me organise my project statement into a clearer and more structured format, making the overall concept, method, and intended impact easier to understand. It was useful in helping me connect ideas that previously felt scattered and confirming the general direction of my project. However, after reading through the draft, I realised that some parts were still underdeveloped, especially the physical making process, the user experience, and the connection between the handmade book and QR interaction, which need more specific detail. I also noticed that some sentences felt too formal, overly broad, and somewhat AI-like, as they did not fully reflect my own writing voice or the specific qualities of my project. This made me realise that while AI can help organise ideas, relying on it too much might influence my own thinking and writing style, so I still need to revise critically and make sure the final statement feels more personal and specific. Moving forward, I need to research further into physical pop-up structures, QR interaction, and better ways to communicate emotional data through interaction. I will continue developing a handmade interactive book that combines physical touch and digital response to create small moments of joy.


*NotebookLM - Peer Share*
![alt text](../assets/week-09/image-6.png) 
During the peer share, my classmate responded to the NotebookLM-generated draft by saying that the overall explanation of the project was clear and compelling, especially the way the project concept and QR code interaction were described. This showed me that the draft was effective in communicating the main idea of the project. However, they also pointed out that some parts still need further development, particularly the future scenario and the inclusion of more personal examples. This feedback helped me realise that although the draft is structured and understandable, it still needs to feel more specific, personal, and connected to my own voice rather than staying too general.


*Making Sprint*
![alt text](../assets/week-09/image-7.png)
For the making sprint, I used my Week 7 documentation as a starting point to sketch a possible physical book layout. Instead of focusing only on digital records, I explored how weekly moments and personal data could be organised visually into a page-based physical format. This sketch helped me test the arrangement of content, the balance between different sections, and how information might be structured across a handmade book page. By translating my earlier documentation into a physical layout draft, I was able to begin thinking more practically about how the book could function as a tactile and interactive object in the next stage of development.


*Round Robin Rapid Reactions*
![alt text](../assets/week-09/image-8.png)
During the Round Robin Rapid Reactions, feedback suggested that my project statement clearly presents a provocation by challenging traditional ways of visualising data and exploring emotional data in a more personal and tactile format. The making sprint outcome also showed that the project is moving more clearly towards a handmade physical book with stronger attention to page structure and interaction. Viewers responded to the project as something reflective and calming, as it focuses on everyday emotions and small moments of joy rather than analytical data alone. However, feedback also highlighted that several aspects still need to be resolved before the Week 12 showcase, particularly the final page development, interaction design, and overall physical consistency of the handmade book.

## AI Usage Statement
![alt text](../assets/week-09/image-9.png)

I used ChatGPT to help generate p5.js code for the QR code interaction prototype. I explained the interaction flow I wanted, including the mood rating page, emoji buttons, and the joke/quote response pages. ChatGPT helped me produce code quickly, which was useful because I did not need to build everything from the beginning by myself. However, the first version was not fully successful, so I had to revise my prompts several times and adjust the code through multiple iterations. This process showed me that AI was helpful for technical prototyping, but it still required my own testing, decision-making, and refinement to make the interaction fit my project.
